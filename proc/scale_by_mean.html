scale_by_mean

private pure function scale_by_mean(x_q32, mean) result(r)

Multiply a Q32.32 value by an integer mean and drop the fraction.

Done in two halves so that nothing overflows. The integer part of x_q32 is at most 9ln(4096), under 75, so the first product cannot exceed 75 * huge(int32). The fractional part is below 232 and mean is at most huge(int32), so the second product is at most (232 - 1) * (2*31 - 1); adding the rounding term below still leaves it under huge(int64), with about 4e9 to spare.

The fraction is rounded to nearest rather than truncated. Truncating would lose on average half a unit per draw, which is invisible at a mean of 4000 ticks and a 23% error at a mean of 2.

Rounding shrinks that but does not remove it. Discretising an exponential to integers leaves E[round(mean*X)] at exp(-1/(2*mean)) / (1 - exp(-1/mean)), which is 4.0% low at a mean of 1, 1.0% at 2, 0.46% at 3 and 0.04% at 10. Above about 100 it is gone. That is inherent to an integer result, not a defect in the scaling, but a caller working in units that small should know the mean it gets is not quite the mean it asked for.

   pure function scale_by_mean(x_q32, mean) result(r)
      !! Multiply a Q32.32 value by an integer mean and drop the fraction.
      !!
      !! Done in two halves so that nothing overflows. The integer part of
      !! `x_q32` is at most 9*ln(4096), under 75, so the first product cannot
      !! exceed 75 * huge(int32). The fractional part is below 2**32 and
      !! `mean` is at most huge(int32), so the second product is at most
      !! (2**32 - 1) * (2**31 - 1); adding the rounding term below still
      !! leaves it under huge(int64), with about 4e9 to spare.
      !!
      !! The fraction is rounded to nearest rather than truncated. Truncating
      !! would lose on average half a unit per draw, which is invisible at a
      !! mean of 4000 ticks and a 23% error at a mean of 2.
      !!
      !! Rounding shrinks that but does not remove it. Discretising an
      !! exponential to integers leaves `E[round(mean*X)]` at
      !! `exp(-1/(2*mean)) / (1 - exp(-1/mean))`, which is 4.0% low at a mean
      !! of 1, 1.0% at 2, 0.46% at 3 and 0.04% at 10. Above about 100 it is
      !! gone. That is inherent to an integer result, not a defect in the
      !! scaling, but a caller working in units that small should know the
      !! mean it gets is not quite the mean it asked for.
      integer(int64), intent(in) :: x_q32
         !! Deviate in Q32.32 fixed point.
      integer(int64), intent(in) :: mean
         !! Mean, in `[0, EXPONENTIAL_MAX_MEAN]`.
      integer(int64) :: r

      r = u64_shr(x_q32, 32_default_int)*mean &
          + u64_shr(iand(x_q32, Q32_MASK)*mean + Q32_HALF, 32_default_int)
   end function scale_by_mean
